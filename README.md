This project explores historical trends and patterns in Nobel Prize awards using a data analysis notebook environment, likely built with DataLab or a similar platform.

The analysis investigates key attributes and insights from the Nobel Prize dataset, such as:

- Distribution of Nobel Prizes by **category**
- Trends in awards over **time**
- Geographical patterns based on **birth countries**
- Gender breakdown among **laureates**

The interactive notebook provides visual insights and summary statistics designed to highlight how the Nobel Prize has evolved since its inception.

## 📌 Findings

- 🏅  Nobel Prizes by Category
Most awarded categories: Physics, Chemistry, and Medicine.

Peace and Literature also receive regular awards but show more variability over the years.

- 📈 Prizes Over Time
A general upward trend in the number of Nobel Prizes awarded per year.

Disruptions observed during major world events (e.g., World Wars), where some years have no or fewer awards.

- 🌍 Laureates by Country of Birth
USA, UK, and Germany dominate Nobel Prize wins by birth country.

Clear global imbalance, developed Western countries are significantly overrepresented.

- 👩‍🔬 Gender Distribution
A stark gender gap: the majority of laureates are male.

Female laureates have gradually increased, especially in Peace and Literature, but remain underrepresented in Sciences.

- 🏫 Affiliated Institutions
Top institutions (e.g., Harvard, MIT, Stanford) have disproportionately high numbers of affiliated laureates.

Suggests strong institutional support and research infrastructure are critical factors.

- 🧠 Age of Laureates
Most Nobel Prizes are awarded to individuals in their 40s to 60s, suggesting a long research gestation period before recognition.
